## Article II — The Executive Branch

The **Vesting Clause**: *"The executive Power shall be vested in a President of the United States of America."* The scope of that vesting is the central interpretive battle of modern presidential power doctrine — the **"unitary executive" theory**.

### Enumerated powers

- **Commander-in-Chief** of the armed forces
- **Treaty-making** (with Senate, two-thirds)
- **Appointment** (with Senate, simple majority)
- **Pardon power** — essentially unlimited federally
- **Take Care Clause** — the duty to execute the laws

Four-year terms, 22nd Amendment caps at two terms.

### The administrative presidency

The modern presidency is largely an **administrative presidency**: the executive branch comprises ~4 million employees across 15 cabinet departments and dozens of independent agencies.

Until 2024, *Chevron v. NRDC* (1984) established judicial deference to agency interpretation of ambiguous statutes — a doctrine that was the operative backbone of the modern regulatory state for forty years. (See the 2024 Administrative-Law Revolution section.)

### Informal powers

- **Executive orders** — no constitutional basis but pervasive
- **Signing statements**
- **Bully pulpit / agenda-setting**
- **Prosecutorial discretion**

<Callout>
  War powers in practice have flowed heavily to the executive since 1945, despite the Constitution's assignment of the *declaration* power to Congress. The War Powers Resolution (1973) has been routinely ignored. The doctrinal gap between Article I, §8 ("declare war") and post-1945 practice is wide enough to drive a fleet of carriers through.
</Callout>
